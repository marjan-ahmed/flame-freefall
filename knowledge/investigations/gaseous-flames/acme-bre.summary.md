ACME BRE used gas-fed porous burners to emulate burning condensed materials
in quiescent microgravity, in the ISS Combustion Integrated Rack, in two
rounds: Feb. to April 2019 and Sept. 2020 to Jan. 2021. Its results are
preliminary by the report's own statement.

- **Atmospheres targeted:** 14.7 psia / 21 % O₂, 10.2 psia / 26.5 % O₂,
  and 8.2 psia / 34 % O₂; burners 25 and 50 mm; no air flow.
- **Quasi-steady burning** was observed for ambient oxygen mole fractions of
  0.265 and above; methane flames self-extinguished within a minute.
- **Radiative loss** reached about 60% in microgravity versus 30% in normal
  gravity; extinction occurred in the 1100 to 1200 K range.
- **PMMA:** one steady flame at 26.5% O₂ and 10.2 psia "could represent PMMA",
  meaning a common plastic could steadily burn in the proposed atmospheres,
  even without the ventilation shut-off used to put out spacecraft fires.
- **Caveat:** test 19070D1 is given two different heats of combustion
  (24 and 22 kJ/g) in consecutive figure captions.
