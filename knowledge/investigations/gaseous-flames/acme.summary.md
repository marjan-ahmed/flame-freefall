ACME ran six gaseous-fuel laminar flame experiments on the ISS Combustion
Integrated Rack — over 1,000 flames ignited across 3.5 years of testing since
September 2017. This entry is sourced from NASA's own poster of selected
results, not a full technical report.

- **CLD Flame**: microgravity flames carry 5–8x more soot, are taller/wider.
- **Burning Rate Emulator**: self-extinguish below ~25% O₂; larger flames
  self-extinguish within 1.5 minutes even at elevated O₂; methane
  self-extinguishes within a minute, ethylene can burn for minutes.
- **Flame Design**: large flames grow until radiative heat loss extinguishes
  them, via a growing oscillatory partial-extinguish/reform pattern.
- **s-Flame**: both radiative (high flow) and kinetic (low flow) extinction
  observed; helium dilution shifts the limits.
- **E-FIELD Flames**: weak electric fields can stabilize highly diluted
  flames; strong fields destabilize jet flames into corona/arc discharges.
- **CFI-G**: no findings reported in this source.
- Different geometry class from BASS-II/SAFFIRE (solid-fuel) and FLEX
  (droplet) — not directly comparable to either without noting that.
