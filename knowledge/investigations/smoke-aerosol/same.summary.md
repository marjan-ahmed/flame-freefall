SAME measured the size of smoke particles from overheated (not burning)
spacecraft materials in the ISS Microgravity Science Glovebox in September
and early October of 2007, to inform future smoke detectors.

- **Samples:** 30 — six each of Teflon, Kapton, silicone rubber, cellulose
  (lamp wick) and dibutyl-phthalate (DBP) as a reference.
- **Conditions:** heated below the ignition point; air flow rates ranging
  from 2 to 8 cm/s; smoke aged from 10 seconds to 1800 seconds.
- **Method:** the moment method, measuring three moments of the size
  distribution with small instruments and assuming a lognormal distribution.
- **Findings:** a substantial portion of the smoke particles are below 500
  nm; Teflon and Kapton smoke is small, lamp wick and silicone larger; aging
  grows the particles; the space shuttle and ISS detectors respond differently
  to particle size.
- **Caveats:** the source does not label its three diameter columns
  individually; the TEM grids were contaminated.
- Smoke / aerosol class: not comparable with any flame measurement.
