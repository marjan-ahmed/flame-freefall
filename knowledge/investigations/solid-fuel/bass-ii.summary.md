BASS-II tested solid-fuel combustion in the ISS Microgravity Science Glovebox
across O₂ concentrations, flow velocities, and fuel geometries. This entry
covers the full report: the overview plus all six PI sub-reports.

- **Overview**: 126 tests (103 planned), ~21% down to ~14% O₂, up to ~55 cm/s
  flow. HRR well below 100 W typically; combustion comparatively inefficient
  (CO:CO₂ well above the 0.004 efficient-flue-gas threshold); global
  equivalence ratio 1.3 (fuel-rich) across 34 returned samples.
- **Bhattacharjee**: extinction velocity is independent of fuel thickness
  (~1 cm/s at 21% O₂, ~2.6–4 cm/s at 16% O₂); de Ris flame coefficient
  F = 4.76 measured directly from ISS data, and 1g downward spread agrees
  with microgravity data within uncertainty.
- **Nomex®**: none of three samples ignited — "an excellent result for fire
  safety."
- **Fernandez-Pello**: flame spread sustained down to 17.5% O₂ in
  microgravity vs. 19% in 1g; full Table 4.1/4.2 rod test conditions included.
- **Miller/Wichman**: full 20-test flat-PMMA matrix (Table 5.1); two-sided
  burns spread faster than one-sided; thicker fuel = slower spread.
- **Olson**: NASA Test 1's ~25 s fixed ignition time is shorter than several
  successful microgravity ignitions observed here (up to 230 s) — a direct
  challenge to the certification test's validity.
- **T'ien**: full 31-test SIBAL fabric matrix (Table 7.1); quenching speeds
  1–5 cm/s, higher in lower-O₂ atmosphere.
- **Appendix A** (six ~20–30-row per-PI raw test matrices) exists but its
  column headers were lost in PDF conversion and are not reliably
  reconstructable — flagged rather than guessed at.
- Same geometry class as SAFFIRE (large-scale solid-fuel burns) — the two are
  this corpus's first directly comparable pair.
