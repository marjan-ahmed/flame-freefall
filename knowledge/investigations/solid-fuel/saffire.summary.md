Saffire VI — the sixth and final flight of NASA's Spacecraft Fire Safety
Demonstration Project — is the primary subject of this entry. The earlier
Saffire-1/Saffire-2 flights (2017 report) are included as background.

- **Saffire VI**: large samples (41 cm wide) burned in an unoccupied Cygnus
  vehicle at much lower pressure (54–55 kPa) and higher O₂ (29–31%) than the
  earlier flights — 1.5–3.2 kW fires; smoke/gas, not heat, was the dominant
  hazard. Both PMMA tests exceeded the 1-hour CO₂ limit and 24-hour CO limit;
  a 1 mg/m³ smoke alarm threshold wasn't reached until 981 s, by which point
  most hazardous gas had already been released.
- **Background — Saffire-1**: 40.6 × 94 cm SIBAL fabric, 20 cm/s flow, 21.7%
  O₂ — spread 1.8 mm/s concurrent / 1.3 mm/s opposed, ~1200 ± 300 W average
  flame power.
- **Background — Saffire-2**: smaller (5 × 29 cm) samples at 22.1% O₂ — none
  of the silicone samples burned in microgravity even though most burned
  readily on Earth; Nomex® (rated "safe") would not ignite even under a
  sustained pilot flame from an adjacent burning PMMA strip.
- Even within the Saffire series, conditions (pressure, O₂) shifted flight to
  flight — a reminder to check conditions before comparing any two results.
- Same geometry class as BASS-II (large-scale solid-fuel burns) — the two are
  this corpus's first directly comparable pair.
