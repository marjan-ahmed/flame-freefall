FLEX burned single spherical droplets of methanol and n-heptane aboard the
ISS Combustion Integrated Rack — spherical symmetry that's only achievable in
microgravity, since normal-gravity convection destroys it. This entry covers
the full apparatus, procedure, measurement uncertainty, and the complete
per-test results table (Table IX), not just the Executive Summary.

- **275 individually tabulated tests** (of 284 performed; 5 test numbers are
  absent from the source's own table) — droplets 2–5 mm, pressures 0.70–3.05
  atm (most at 0.7–1 atm), oxygen diluted with nitrogen, carbon dioxide,
  and/or helium, each with its own ambient composition, burn time, extinction
  diameter, burning rate, and end condition (Extinction/Disruption/Completion).
- **More than 100 useful combustion histories** obtained; radiative,
  diffusive, and disruptive extinction all observed for both fuels.
- **Measurement uncertainty by camera**: HiBMs (droplet) ±50 µm, LLUV (flame,
  OH• edge) ±200 µm, MDCA color camera (flame, visible edge) ±250 µm —
  color-camera flame diameters run ~3/4 of LLUV's for the same test.
- **Data quality caveat**: a conformal coating on the fuel needles flaked into
  some droplets; the source's own 8-point assessment says burning-rate/flame
  history were minimally affected, but methanol disruptive extinction was
  "probably" contaminant-driven (heptane's is not attributable either way).
- **The report deliberately does not interpret its own data** — it exists to
  archive results for future papers to analyze. Appendix B's per-frame
  time-series curves (a different kind of artifact from the per-test table)
  are intentionally not transcribed here.
- Different geometry class from BASS-II/SAFFIRE (solid-fuel) and ACME
  (gaseous flames) — not directly comparable to either without noting that.
