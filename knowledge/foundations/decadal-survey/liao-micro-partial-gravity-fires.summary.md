Ya-Ting Liao's decadal-survey white paper argues fire safety research must
advance for Artemis/Mars-era missions, where partial gravity and proposed
high-oxygen Lunar atmospheres compound spacecraft fire risk.

- Microgravity eliminates buoyant flow; spacecraft HVAC's ~10–20 cm/s forced
  flow instead controls fire behavior, and steady flame spread rate depends
  on flow velocity, pressure, O₂%, confinement, sample thickness, and
  arrangement.
- States the corpus problem directly: NASA's PSI repository "is far from
  completion... combustion investigations before the 2000s are missing," and
  limited camera-based diagnostics "prevent direct comparisons between
  different experiments."
- Its fourth research recommendation calls explicitly for a **comprehensive
  fire database** to accelerate model/theory development and enable machine
  learning applications — the exact gap this corpus is built to address.
- The extinction boundary has two limits: a low-velocity **quenching limit**
  (heat generation ≈ heat loss) and a high-velocity **blowoff limit**
  (insufficient reactant residence time); raising O₂% lowers the former and
  raises the latter.
- In partial gravity, proposed atmospheres get specific: Lunar modules ~34%
  O₂ / 56.5 kPa, Lunar Gateway ~26.5% O₂ / 73.5 kPa — and partial-gravity fire
  behavior can't be assumed interpolable from normal- and micro-gravity data.
- Scale modeling (Froude modeling, pressure modeling, reduced-pressure
  simulation at ~20–40 kPa) is one proposed path to predictive tools, but the
  paper flags real limits to how far low-pressure stands in for low-gravity.
