DAFT investigated whether a modified P-Trak could operate in low gravity,
where condensate control was uncertain. A P-Trak and Dust Trak flew on
Expedition 10; a reference source followed on Expedition 13.

The cabin-air campaign reports averages below 0.005 mg/m3 for Dust Trak and
15 particles /cm3 for P-Trak. These bounds describe campaign averages, not
instrument detection limits or a universal alarm setting. Velcro separation
was an intentional aerosol-generating event, not a combustion test.

Reference dust was sampled over 3 runs. The presentation reports good
comparison in the first run, before low alcohol affected the measurements,
and concludes that modified-instrument functionality in low gravity was
demonstrated. Identified sampling histories, calibration information and a
quantitative agreement criterion are still needed for further analysis.
